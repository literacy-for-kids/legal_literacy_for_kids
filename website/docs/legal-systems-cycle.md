# Legal Systems Cycle

This page shows the full cycle that the curriculum keeps returning to:

**Rule -> Interpretation -> Enforcement -> Dispute -> Evidence -> Ruling -> Appeal -> Amendment**

The course does not teach these as isolated topics. It teaches them as one repeating system.

## Learner-Facing Version

Here is the short version to use with students:

1. **Rule** - Someone writes or agrees to a rule.
2. **Interpretation** - People decide what the rule means in real situations.
3. **Enforcement** - Someone checks whether the rule was followed.
4. **Dispute** - People disagree about what happened or what the rule requires.
5. **Evidence** - The system asks what facts, documents, or observations support each side.
6. **Ruling** - A decision-maker applies the rule and explains the outcome.
7. **Appeal** - A later review checks whether the process was followed correctly.
8. **Amendment** - The system patches the rule or document after new problems show up.

## Facilitator-Facing Version

Use this version when you want a little more precision.

| Stage | What happens | Core learner question |
|---|---|---|
| Rule | A law, agreement, policy, charter, or norm is written or adopted. | Who made this, and what is it for? |
| Interpretation | Real situations expose ambiguity, edge cases, and competing readings. | What do these words mean here? |
| Enforcement | A person or institution applies the rule. | Who checks, and what happens if it breaks? |
| Dispute | Someone says the rule was misread, misapplied, or broken. | Who disagrees, and about what? |
| Evidence | The system looks for reasons, records, witnesses, and facts. | What supports each side? |
| Ruling | A decision is made and explained. | What rule controlled, and why? |
| Appeal | A higher or later review checks the process. | Was the system fair and consistent? |
| Amendment | The document or process is revised. | What should we patch next time? |

## Where the 18 Core Weeks Fit

| Weeks | Main focus in the cycle |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1-3** | Why rules exist, how shared systems fail, and what foundational rules are for |
| **Weeks 4-7** | How agreements are designed, specified, and negotiated |
| **Weeks 8-10** | Interpretation, loopholes, and precedent |
| **Weeks 11-14** | Rights, power, governance, charter design, and amendment logic |
| **Weeks 15-18** | Courts, due process, evidence, rulings, appeals, and final amendments |

## Detailed Arc by Unit

### Weeks 1-3: Why Rules Exist

Students start with the question of why systems need rules at all. They move from chaos and shared-resource collapse to the first laws of a new society.

### Weeks 4-7: Agreements and Contracts

Students learn how precise agreements are designed. They practice offer, acceptance, consideration, definitions, outputs, edge cases, and revisions.

### Weeks 8-10: Interpretation, Loopholes, and Precedent

Students learn that rules do not apply themselves. Someone has to interpret them, close loopholes, and live with the consequences of earlier rulings.

### Weeks 11-14: Rights, Power, and the Micro-Charter

Students design a governing document with rights, rules, procedures, and amendment mechanisms. Then they stress-test it and patch what breaks.

### Weeks 15-18: Courts, Due Process, Trial, Appeal

Students look at how disputes are handled when systems fail to resolve themselves. They study courts, standards of proof, fair procedures, written rulings, appeals, and revision.

## Optional Advanced Weeks

The optional weeks deepen the cycle instead of interrupting it.

- **Criminal vs. Civil Law** adds parallel court tracks and different burdens of proof.
- **International Law** shows what happens when the system has no single top-level enforcer.

## Why This Map Matters

Many learners think law is just a pile of commands. This map helps them see law as a living process.

It also helps facilitators keep the pacing grounded. If a student gets stuck, ask where you are in the cycle:

- Are you still designing the rule?
- Are you interpreting the text?
- Are you gathering evidence?
- Are you revising the system after a failure?

That question usually tells you what to do next.
