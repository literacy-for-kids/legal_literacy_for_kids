# Executive Function Skills for Rules, Rights, and Agreements

Legal literacy uses careful organization: facts, rules, questions, terms, agreements, evidence, and steps. Executive function helps kids keep those pieces visible while learning legal ideas.

This page is the local doorway into the Literacy for Kids [Executive Function Toolkit](https://www.literacy-for-kids.com/docs/toolkits/executive-function), connected to the careful reasoning this curriculum builds.

## A few core ideas

- **Rules and agreements are easier when terms are clear.** Name the pieces before reasoning about them.
- **Facts, rules, and questions need organization.** Sorting them stops the juggling.
- **Checklists help with multi-step processes.** Steps in order are easier to follow.
- **Real legal problems need trusted adult or professional help.** Organizing learning is not legal advice.

## When this shows up

- When learning legal terms
- When sorting facts from rules
- When an agreement has several parts
- When a process has steps
- When a pretend case or mock trial needs organization
- When questions need trusted adult support

## Tools that help

- **Facts / rules / questions checklist** — three columns, sorted.
- **Term/example card** — a word paired with a pretend example.
- **Agreement checklist** — the parts to look for.
- **Mock trial role checklist** — who does what.
- **Trusted adult / professional help** — for real problems.

:::tip Executive Function Moment
Rules and agreements get easier when the pieces are visible. Try a three-part checklist: facts, rules, questions. Sorting the pieces helps your brain stop juggling everything at once.
:::

:::caution Everyday task skills, not legal advice
These are everyday planning and organization tools for learning legal ideas, not legal advice. For real legal problems, involve a trusted adult or qualified professional. Kids should not be treated as lazy or broken when starting, organizing, or finishing is hard.
:::
