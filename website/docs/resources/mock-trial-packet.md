# Mock Trial Packet

## Purpose

This packet gives learners and facilitators a complete low-stakes structure for the mock trial.

## When to Use It

- Week 17
- Any later review activity that uses the Charter court

## Student Directions

Start with the complete fictional **Missing Markers** case below. It supplies the dispute, rules, party statements, witness cards, exhibits, procedure, and facilitator notes. No real conflict or additional case writing is needed. Read the common materials, let both sides prepare, and use the forms below to record the hearing.

## Facilitator Notes

### Safety and Suitability Protocol

- Use low-stakes disputes only.
- Do not use bullying, abuse, trauma, serious harm, exclusion with real emotional stakes, family secrets, money pressure, romantic conflict, or intense relationship history.
- All participants must opt in.
- A facilitator may veto or replace any scenario.
- The goal is fair-process practice, not punishment or embarrassment.
- If the real dispute feels heavy, switch to fiction immediately.
- Do not simulate police questioning, interrogation, criminal punishment, or real family conflict.
- Do not ask learners to reveal private legal experiences.

## Ready-to-Run Case: The Missing Markers

**Everything in this case is invented.** The rules and proof standard are a fictional club's classroom procedures, not statements of real law or real court requirements. Participants may observe, read cards, use AAC, or pass. Do not assign roles based on real disputes or a learner's reputation.

### Case Summary for Everyone

The Maple Model Club held a drawing session on Tuesday. Casey borrowed the club's six markers. At 2:00 p.m., Morgan, the supply keeper, could not find them in the marker tub and found no return entry on the sign-out sheet. Morgan says Casey failed to return them. Casey says they were returned to the supplies table before leaving.

The markers were later found in the drawing tray at a display table. The dispute is whether Casey followed the return rule, whether the missing log entry matters separately, and what response fits the evidence. There is no theft allegation, criminal case, injury, or real financial loss.

### Rule Card R — The Club Rules in Force That Day

1. **Return location:** “Return borrowed markers to the supplies table before leaving the session.” The rules do not specify a container on that table.
2. **Return record:** “Write the return time in the sign-out sheet when you return borrowed markers.”
3. **Moving materials:** “A club helper may move completed drawing trays to the display table. Check for borrowed supplies and tell the supply keeper where any supplies went.”
4. **Fair hearing:** Share the complaint, response, rule, and exhibits with both sides. Each side may explain, ask relevant questions, and say what they do not know. Use respectful turn-taking; no intimidation or invented evidence.
5. **Proof and uncertainty:** For this classroom club, the person making a claim must support it as **more likely than not**. Decide each alleged rule breach separately. If the evidence is evenly balanced, say “not established” instead of assuming a breach. This is the case's chosen teaching rule, not a universal legal standard.
6. **Response and review:** Use a proportionate, non-punitive response such as updating a record, returning an item, or clarifying a procedure. A second look may address relevant new evidence or a process or rule-application mistake. New wording applies to future sessions, not retroactively.

### Party Card — Morgan, Person Bringing the Complaint

**Agreed facts:** Casey borrowed six markers. At 2:00 I found no markers in the marker tub and no return time on the sheet. All six were found later.

**My claim:** Casey did not follow rules 1 and 2. I checked the tub, not every part of the supplies table. I did not see Casey leave or place the markers.

**Requested response:** Casey should help correct the return record and help label where markers belong before the next session. I initially assumed the empty tub meant no return; I am willing to reconsider that with evidence.

**Preparation task:** Cite the exhibit or witness for each allegation. Distinguish what Morgan saw from what Morgan inferred. Do not add facts about Casey's past behavior.

### Party Card — Casey, Person Responding

**Agreed facts:** I borrowed the six markers and did not write a return time. I left at about 1:52, before the session ended.

**My account:** At about 1:50, I put all six capped markers in the drawing tray on the supplies table. I thought that met rule 1. I forgot the separate log step in rule 2.

**Requested response:** Correct the missing record, but do not call my return a rule-1 breach just because the tub was empty later. I can help label a return container for next time.

**Preparation task:** Point to the exact wording of rule 1 and relevant evidence. Admit the missing entry rather than arguing that it proves nothing at all.

### Witness Card W1 — Robin, Observer

**What I directly saw:** Around 1:50, I saw Casey put six capped markers in a drawing tray on the supplies table. The markers looked like the club set. I did not inspect each marker's label. Casey left shortly afterward.

**What I did not see:** I did not watch the table continuously afterward or see who moved the tray. I did not see anyone write in the return log.

**How to answer:** Read these facts. If asked who moved the tray, say “I did not see that.” Do not invent extra observations.

### Witness Card W2 — Alex, Club Helper

**What I directly did and saw:** At about 1:55, I carried the drawing tray from the supplies table to the display table. I was looking at the drawings and did not check inside for supplies or tell Morgan about markers. At 2:10, Morgan and I found six club markers inside that tray.

**What I do not know:** I did not see who first put the markers there. I cannot give an exact minute for Casey's return.

**How to answer:** Acknowledge the missed helper check under rule 3. This hearing does not turn Alex into a new respondent without notice. The group can discuss a future procedure fix, but cannot issue an unannounced allegation against Alex.

### Evidence Exhibits for Both Sides

**E1 — Sign-out sheet (fictional original club record)**

| Borrower | Item | Borrowed at | Returned at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Casey | Six club markers | 1:15 p.m. | Blank |

The blank records an omitted step. By itself it does not prove the physical markers were never returned.

**E2 — Supplies-table layout recorded before the session**

| Table area | Item present |
|---|---|
| Left | Labeled marker tub |
| Center | Sign-out sheet |
| Right | Drawing tray |

The drawing tray was on the supplies table. This layout and R1 are available to both sides; no photo or improvised prop is required.

**E3 — Morgan's 2:00 note**

“At 2:00 the marker tub was empty. Casey's return-time space was blank. I did not check where the drawing tray had gone.”

This is evidence of the check Morgan made, not a complete observation of the earlier return.

**E4 — Recovery note signed by Morgan and Alex at 2:10**

“We found six markers with the club's label in the drawing tray at the display table. They were capped and undamaged. We moved them back to the marker tub.”

Recovery establishes the later location and condition; it does not alone identify who put them there or when.

**E5 — Session schedule**

“Drawing session: 1:00–2:00 p.m. Helpers may move completed drawings to the display table before cleanup.”

Use R3 to check what a helper should do when moving a tray. Do not invent a rule that nobody may leave before 2:00.

### Completed Complaint and Response Starters

| Item | Complaint | Response |
|---|---|---|
| R1 return location | Empty tub at 2:00 suggests no return | R1 says supplies table; W1 places markers in its drawing tray before Casey left |
| R2 return record | E1 is blank | Casey agrees the entry was missed |
| Requested response | Correct log and label a return place | Agree to repair the record; distinguish R1 from R2 |
| Remaining uncertainty | Morgan did not observe the return | W1 did not inspect labels; W2 did not see who placed them |

### Role Cards and Small-Group Options

- **Judge or panel:** Apply the supplied rules to each claim; say what is established, uncertain, and outside this hearing.
- **Morgan's speaker:** Read Morgan's card and cite evidence. Strong volume or dramatic performance earns no extra credit.
- **Casey's speaker:** Read Casey's card, acknowledge the log omission, and explain the location argument.
- **Witness reader:** Read W1 and W2, including limits. Say “not known” when a question goes beyond a card.
- **Evidence keeper:** Make R and E1–E5 available to both sides; track which item supports each assertion.

For **one adult and one learner**, the adult reads the party and witness cards without improvising; the learner organizes evidence and proposes a decision. For **solo analysis**, read both sides, fill the evidence table, and draft a decision. Do not require a learner to accuse a real peer or defend personal behavior.

### Hearing Script (About 20 Minutes After Preparation)

1. **Notice and preparation:** Before the hearing, give both sides R, all party/witness cards, and E1–E5. Allow preparation and explain accessible response choices.
2. **State the dispute (2 minutes):** Judge reads the two alleged rule breaches and the proof rule. Ask whether everyone has the same materials.
3. **Hear both accounts (4 minutes):** Each speaker states their account and requested response without interruption.
4. **Read and question evidence (6 minutes):** Read W1, W2, and relevant exhibits. Each side may ask a question through the judge. No witness may add facts outside the cards.
5. **Summarize (2 minutes):** Each side identifies its strongest evidence and one uncertainty.
6. **Decide (4 minutes):** Judge separates R1 and R2, gives reasons, and names a proportionate response. Use the short ruling form below.
7. **Debrief (2 minutes):** Did both sides get notice, the same evidence, a chance to respond, and a reasoned decision? Record a future rule clarification for Week 18.

### Facilitator Reasoning Notes

A well-supported sample conclusion is:

- **R1 breach not established:** Casey's account, W1, the table layout, and Alex's tray movement support a return to the supplies table before Casey left. The empty tub at 2:00 does not negate that earlier return. R1 did not require the marker tub. A learner can discuss witness uncertainty, but should not invent a stricter container rule or treat missing proof as proof of guilt.
- **R2 breach established:** E1 is blank and Casey agrees no entry was made. That is a separate recording omission, even if the physical return occurred.
- **Response:** Update the log honestly, using “about 1:50, reported later” rather than fabricating a contemporaneous entry. Label a future return container and remind helpers to check trays. No criminal language or punishment is needed.
- **Future amendment:** “Return markers to the labeled tub on the supplies table and record the time. Helpers check trays before moving them.” Do not use this new wording to judge the earlier return.

Assess the link between rule, evidence, uncertainty, and response, not whether every learner uses identical wording. If learners propose a different conclusion, require a coherent explanation using the existing record and the chosen proof rule. Discuss how a process error could affect the result; simply disliking the answer is not the packet's second-look standard.

### Week 18 Second-Look Variation

After recording the first ruling, use one optional **process** variation: “The judge relied only on E3 and never allowed Casey to see W1.” Ask whether review is warranted. **Yes:** a relevant chance to see and answer evidence was omitted. Reopen that part of the hearing with both sides and all original materials; review does not guarantee a different final answer.

Do not add surprise evidence midway through the original hearing. Any new information must be shared with both sides with time to respond.

## Optional Adaptation Ideas

Use these only after writing a complete fictional rule, both accounts, witness limits, exhibits, and answer notes. The default case above needs no adaptation.

- a shared project table was left messy
- someone used the group's markers without returning them
- a reserved reading spot was used during someone else's slot
- a game-night rule about turns was not followed
- a server or club rule about quiet planning time was broken
- a library checkout reminder was misunderstood
- a community-center sign was read two different ways

## Civil Discussion Moves

- "Can you explain what you mean by...?"
- "What evidence or example supports that?"
- "What rule or process matters here?"
- "I see it differently because..."
- "I need a moment before I answer."

## Order of Proceeding Checklist

1. Notice given
2. Time to prepare provided
3. Evidence shared in advance
4. Access needs, translation, AAC, or visual supports prepared
5. Hearing begins
6. Opening statements
7. Complaining side presents
8. Responding side replies
9. Judge or panel questions
10. Closing statements
11. Decision discussion
12. Written or spoken ruling
13. Amendment note if the Charter exposed a gap

## Younger Learner Adaptation

- Use one-page complaint and one-page response only.
- Skip formal opening and closing statements.
- Use drawings or objects as evidence.

## Older Learner Extension

- Add a proof-job note.
- Require each side to quote the Charter text directly.
- Add a post-trial section on whether a second-look issue exists.
- Compare two sources if the case depends on a message, screenshot, post, or clip.
