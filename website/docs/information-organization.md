# Information Organization Skills for Rules, Rights, and Agreements

Legal literacy depends on keeping facts, rules, questions, examples, evidence, agreements, and roles separate enough to reason carefully. Information Organization helps kids avoid mixing everything into one confusing pile.

This page is the local doorway into the Literacy for Kids [Information Organization Toolkit](https://www.literacy-for-kids.com/docs/toolkits/information-organization), connected to the careful reasoning this curriculum builds.

## A few core ideas

- **Facts, rules, questions, and opinions are different kinds of information.** Careful reasoning starts with sorted piles.
- **Agreements need labels for roles and responsibilities.** Who agreed to what should be visible.
- **Examples and non-examples help legal ideas make sense.** Tricky cases mark the boundaries.
- **Evidence should stay connected to its source.** A claim without a source is hard to test.
- **Real legal problems need trusted adult or professional help.**

## When this shows up

- When sorting facts from rules
- When reading an agreement
- When learning a legal term
- When preparing a pretend case
- When organizing evidence
- When asking questions about rights or responsibilities

## Tools that help

- **Facts / rules / questions sort** — three columns before arguing.
- **Agreement role label** — who is responsible for which part.
- **Example / non-example** — for legal terms and tricky cases.
- **Evidence/source tracker** — keep the claim connected.
- **Pretend case organizer** — facts, rules, questions, and evidence with homes.

:::tip Information Organization Moment
Legal thinking gets harder when facts, rules, and questions are mixed together. Try three columns: facts, rules, questions. Sorting the information helps the group reason more carefully.
:::

:::caution Everyday organization skills, not legal advice
These are everyday information organization tools for learning about rules, rights, and agreements, not legal advice. For real legal problems, involve a trusted adult or qualified professional. Kids should not be expected to manage unsafe or serious situations alone.
:::
