# Glossary

This glossary collects the key terms used throughout the Legal Literacy for Kids curriculum. Each entry includes a short, kid-friendly definition and the week where the term was first introduced. If a term feels fuzzy, re-read it here, then go back to the week where it lives — the term will land more solidly with the surrounding context.

A reminder: this is an educational systems toolkit, not legal advice. The definitions here are designed to teach clear thinking about rules and systems, not to be cited in a real legal matter.

---

## Terms

| Term | Definition | First Introduced |
|---|---|---|
| **Acceptance** | A clear agreement to the exact terms of an offer, without changes. The "handshake" half of a contract. | Week 4 |
| **Ambiguity** | When a rule could reasonably be read more than one way. Most legal interpretation is the work of resolving ambiguity. | Week 8 |
| **Amendment** | A formal change to a document, usually following a specified procedure. | Week 13 |
| **Anti-circumvention** | Rules specifically designed to stop people from gaming other rules. A defense against loopholes. | Week 9 |
| **Appeal** | A request to a higher court to review a ruling because the process was flawed — not just because you lost. | Week 18 |
| **Beyond reasonable doubt** | The very high standard of proof used in criminal cases. Closer to 99% certain than to 51% certain. | Week 15 |
| **Binding** | Required to be followed; not just a polite intention. Contracts and precedents can both be binding. | Week 4 |
| **Breach** | Failing to follow through on a contractual obligation. | Week 7 |
| **Burden of proof** | The job of proving something in a dispute, and the standard of certainty required to win. | Week 15 |
| **Case Notes** | The running journal kept through all 18 weeks, where the student records rules they noticed and rulings they made. | Intro |
| **Charter** | A foundational document that establishes the rules and structure of a system. | Week 13 |
| **Checks and balances** | A design where each part of a system can correct mistakes in the other parts. | Week 12 |
| **Civil law** | The branch of law where one party sues another over a private harm. Standard of proof: preponderance of evidence. | Optional Week 1 |
| **Commons** | A resource that belongs to everyone and can be used by anyone. | Week 2 |
| **Consideration** | The value each side gives in a contract. Can be a thing, an action, a promise, or even silence. | Week 4 |
| **Contract** | A binding agreement between two or more parties, made up of an offer, an acceptance, and consideration. | Week 4 |
| **Counter-offer** | Changing any term of the offer. This kills the original offer and creates a new one. | Week 5 |
| **Criminal law** | The branch of law where the state prosecutes someone on behalf of society. Standard of proof: beyond reasonable doubt. | Optional Week 1 |
| **Custom** | An unwritten rule that everyone follows anyway, like taking shoes off at the door. | Week 1 |
| **Distinguish** | To argue that a new case is meaningfully different from an old precedent, so the precedent doesn't apply. | Week 10 |
| **Distributed power** | Authority spread across multiple sources so no single one can override the system. | Week 12 |
| **Due process** | The structural guarantee that the right process must be followed before any legal consequence — even when the answer seems obvious. | Week 16 |
| **Edge case** | An unusual situation the basic rule doesn't quite handle. | Week 7 |
| **Enforcement** | How a rule is actually applied. Who checks? What happens if it's broken? | Week 3 |
| **Firewall** | A hardcoded barrier in a system. Rights act as firewalls between institutions and individuals. | Week 11 |
| **Forbearance** | Agreeing to *not* do something you had the right to do. Can count as consideration in a contract. | Week 6 |
| **Foundational law** | A law written at the start of a system, often harder to change than later laws. | Week 3 |
| **Free rider** | Someone who benefits from a shared system without contributing to it. | Week 2 |
| **Gift** | A one-way transfer of value with no consideration in return. Different from a contract. | Week 4 |
| **Intent** | The purpose the rule was designed to serve, as opposed to its exact words. | Week 8 |
| **Interpretation** | The process of figuring out how a rule applies to a specific situation. | Week 8 |
| **Invitation to treat** | A display (like a price tag or menu) that looks like an offer but is actually inviting the customer to make the offer. | Week 5 |
| **Jurisdiction** | Which set of rules applies, and which court has authority to rule on a case. | Week 3 |
| **Law** | A rule written down by a government, with official consequences for breaking it. | Week 1 |
| **Letter (of the law)** | The literal, word-for-word reading of a rule. | Week 8 |
| **Loophole** | A way to follow the letter of a rule while violating its intent. | Week 9 |
| **Majority** | The larger side in a group vote. Majorities usually win, but rights block them on protected issues. | Week 11 |
| **Meeting of the minds** | When both sides actually agree on the same exact terms of a contract. | Week 5 |
| **Micro-Charter** | The foundational document the student writes in Week 13 — a constitution for a real domain they care about. | Week 13 |
| **Mutual obligation** | Both sides of a contract are committed to do something. The defining feature of a contract. | Week 6 |
| **Offer** | A clear, specific statement of what one party will do in exchange for what. | Week 4 |
| **Overturn** | To replace an old precedent with a new ruling. A bigger move than distinguishing. | Week 10 |
| **Patch** | An update to a rule that closes a specific loophole. | Week 9 |
| **Plain meaning** | The most obvious, ordinary reading of a rule's words. | Week 8 |
| **Preamble** | The opening paragraph of a foundational document, stating its purpose. | Week 13 |
| **Precedent** | A past ruling that becomes binding for similar future cases. The version-control system of the legal world. | Week 10 |
| **Predictability** | Knowing roughly what's going to happen next. The thing rules create so that societies can scale. | Week 1 |
| **Preponderance of evidence** | The standard of proof used in civil cases. Closer to 51% certain — "more likely than not." | Week 15 |
| **Procedural due process** | The right process was followed (notice, hearing, evidence, response). | Week 16 |
| **Purpose clause** | A statement in a rule explaining what the rule is intended to accomplish. A defense against loopholes. | Week 9 |
| **Ratify** | To formally approve and adopt a document. | Week 13 |
| **Regulation** | A rule that manages how a shared resource is used, to keep it sustainable. | Week 2 |
| **Remedy** | The agreed-upon consequence when a breach happens — what the wronged party gets. | Week 7 |
| **Right** | A constraint on institutional power that protects an individual, even from majority decisions. | Week 11 |
| **Rule** | An instruction that tells people what to do or not do. | Week 1 |
| **Rule of law** | The principle that the rules apply equally and predictably to everyone, regardless of who they are. | Intro |
| **Separation of powers** | Separating rule-making, enforcement, and interpretation into different bodies. | Week 12 |
| **Single point of failure** | A component whose failure causes the whole system to fail. | Week 12 |
| **Sovereignty** | The principle that each nation's internal law is its own business — a firewall against outside jurisdiction. | Optional Week 2 |
| **Specification** | A precise description of what is being done or provided. The more specific, the fewer disputes. | Week 7 |
| **Spirit of the law** | The general intent and purpose of a rule, as opposed to its exact words. | Week 9 |
| **Standing** | The right to bring a case — usually because you were personally harmed by what happened. | Week 15 |
| **Stare decisis** | Latin for "to stand by decided things." The principle that courts follow precedent. | Week 10 |
| **Substantive due process** | The outcome itself was fair — not just the process used to reach it. | Week 16 |
| **Sustainable** | Something that can keep going without running out. The opposite of "collapsing." | Week 2 |
| **System** | A collection of parts that work together. The legal system is the collection of laws, courts, and procedures. | Week 1 |
| **Tragedy of the commons** | When individuals acting in self-interest overuse a shared resource and destroy it for everyone. | Week 2 |
| **Treaty** | A contract between nations: offer, acceptance, and consideration all apply, but enforcement is much harder. | Optional Week 2 |
| **Unalienable** | Cannot be taken away or transferred, even by majority vote. Often used to describe foundational rights. | Week 11 |
| **Verdict** | The formal judgment in a case — who wins, what remedy applies, and the reasoning behind it. | Week 18 |

## A note on legal terms

These definitions are written for clarity, not for courtrooms. A real lawyer or judge might draw finer distinctions than this glossary does — especially around terms like *standing*, *burden of proof*, *jurisdiction*, and the line between *civil* and *criminal*. If you ever have a real legal question, talk to a real lawyer.

The point of this glossary is not to memorize. It is to give you a shared vocabulary so that you can read a rule, talk about a system, and notice when something is off. That is the foundation. Everything else builds on top of it.
