# Collaboration Skills for Rules, Rights, and Agreements

Legal literacy includes agreements, roles, responsibilities, fairness, evidence, rules, and group processes like mock trials. Collaboration helps kids understand how shared work and shared rules need clarity.

This page is the local doorway into the Literacy for Kids [Collaboration Toolkit](https://www.literacy-for-kids.com/docs/toolkits/collaboration), connected to the careful reasoning this curriculum builds.

## A few core ideas

- **Agreements work better when roles and responsibilities are visible.** Who does what is half of any agreement.
- **Group legal-learning activities need facts, rules, questions, and roles.** Sorting the pieces is shared work.
- **Fair process includes listening and clear expectations.** Process matters, not just outcomes.
- **Real legal problems need trusted adult or professional help.** Learning about agreements is not legal advice.

## When this shows up

- When learning about agreements
- When sorting facts, rules, and questions
- When doing a mock trial
- When discussing fairness or process
- When people disagree about what a rule means
- When a real legal issue appears

## Tools that help

- **Shared goal card** — what is the group trying to figure out?
- **Facts/rules/questions roles** — one tracker for each.
- **Mock trial role check** — speaker, evidence tracker, question asker, timekeeper.
- **Agreement responsibility check** — who agreed to what?
- **Trusted adult / professional help** — for real legal problems.

:::tip Collaboration Moment
A group legal activity works better when roles are clear. Ask: "Who is tracking facts, who is tracking rules, and who is tracking questions?" Shared roles help the group reason carefully.
:::

:::caution Everyday collaboration skills, not legal advice
These are everyday collaboration tools for learning about rules, rights, and agreements, not legal advice. For real legal problems, involve a trusted adult or qualified professional. Kids should not be expected to manage unsafe or serious situations alone.
:::
